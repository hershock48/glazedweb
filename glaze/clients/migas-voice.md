# MI Gas: how he actually writes

Drawn from his own long-form Patreon posts, supplied by Kevin in August 2026:
the non-nitrogen calcium post, the runoff guide, the complete bloom guide, the
callus tech post, and the nutrients and plant biology primer.

**His Patreon is a paid product.** This file records *how* he writes so the site
can sound like him. It is not a place to store his material, and nothing from it
gets lifted onto a public marketing page. Copy that reproduces what he sells
devalues the thing he sells, and it would read to his subscribers as a leak.

---

## The one-line version

**He is a careful, generous, numerate operator who tells you what he measured,
what he was told, and what he is still unsure about, and keeps those three
separate.** Everything below is downstream of that.

---

## The seven traits, in order of how much they matter

### 1. He is specific to the decimal, and that is where his authority comes from

Not "feed a small shot." He writes: 252 ml per feed per plant, 4785 ml of total
substrate, so a 5% shot size, and he prefers 3 to 8%. He gives the arithmetic:
1 gallon is 3785 ml, divide by 60 minutes, so a 2 gph set-up should read 120 to
130 ml in a one-minute test. Day 42 to 48. 1.5 EC by day 44. DLI of 39 to 42.
Netafim .5 gph. Runoff 20 to 30%.

**A grower believes him because he can check him.** Any page that describes what
he does without a single number is describing a consultant who might be anybody.
One real figure outperforms a paragraph of positioning.

### 2. He hedges constantly, on purpose, and it is a strength

This is the most distinctive thing about him and the easiest to flatten by
accident. He continually separates measured from reported from suspected:

- "I regretfully admit that we haven't taken any additional foliar analysis tests
  since we started using them."
- "his words, not gospel. Seen pushback on this elsewhere fwiw."
- "maybe not though, idk."
- "I don't think this is the only way or even necessarily the 'right way.' I know
  I have a lot to learn."
- "it's hard to tell for sure if or how much we've benefited from this."
- "There isn't inherently one right or wrong way to do this."

**Never write him a flat promise.** His whole credibility rests on the fact that
he tells you when he does not know. A site that sells certainty on his behalf
contradicts him in front of the exact audience that can tell.

### 3. "It depends, and here is precisely what it depends on"

He almost never gives a rule without its conditions:

> "It has a lot more to do with your runoff management, how long your flower
> cycle is, and how much nitrogen is still in your leaves and plants leading up
> to this time frame. If your runoff is high because you unintentionally stacked
> and your leaves are dark green at day 42 and you're only flowering 60 days,
> maybe you need to be more aggressive."

That is the shape of the product. The site's existing diagnosis-before-
introduction framing is right; this is why. But note that his version is warmer
and longer than an epigram. He explains the branch, he does not just assert that
one exists.

### 4. Anti-hype, explicitly, and he names it

He devotes real space to it. He defines hype from the dictionary. He calls out
Athena for publishing good information "then lace it with a sales pitch at the
end," and says to take it "with a grain of salt and try to extract out the good
info." He warns that the pendulum overcorrects, that "ideology of any type is a
dangerous game," and that "the truth is usually somewhere in the middle."

**So marketing polish is a liability here, not an asset.** Copy that sounds like
a brochure is the thing he tells his audience to distrust.

### 5. He gives credit relentlessly, including to people who have wronged him

Slow Nickel blocked him and he still writes: "I'll continue to support his work
whether he likes it or not, as we ultimately share the same mission." He credits
Dankems, clone coach, the matsugrower schedule, Matthew Gilbert, Craft, Ramsay
Nubani, Utah State's commercial cannabis programme, and his own veg and mom
manager by handle, telling readers to go follow him.

His stated reason for selling his methods at all:

> "I'm okay with leveling the playing field. And making others around me better
> because it makes me better and prevents me from becoming complacent."

**A consultant who names his sources and his staff is unusual and disarming.**
It is available to the site and currently unused.

### 6. "We", and the team is real

Almost never "I" when describing the operation. There is a veg, mom and clone
manager. There are employees, one of whom took the Utah State course. He thanks
"great facility, team and ownership."

### 7. He owns failures in specific, dated detail

> "We recently had a rough run of clones that were stressed and very slow to
> launch. Some bad communication and a few key people out of town and boom we
> were at day 7 with no roots and stress signs setting into our foliage."

He then says what they learned and that the run would never be their best work.
**A page with no failure in it does not sound like him.**

## Register

Casual, direct, warm, occasionally profane. He addresses the reader: "Frustrating,
right? Welcome to growing." He signs off: "Cheers." "Be well my friends and
continue to grow hard." He says "the homie," "for the dummies," "smart
motherfucker," and drops a parenthetical joke mid-instruction ("yes, we are still
talking about weed here").

He assumes competence and tells you to go look things up: "This education model
is built on the idea that everyone here is roughly intermediate level knowledge
and experience or better. If you're brand new, no worries. Go off with the google
search bar as you go."

He is capable of real lyricism and rations it to the end of a post, after the
technical work has earned it. The callus post closes: *"Two men stand behind
prison bars. One sees mud and the other see stars."* One of those per site, at
most, and only at the end.

**He is not epigrammatic.** He does not write in polished aphorisms. Copy built
out of neat inversions sounds like a good agency, not like him.

## What he treats as proof, which settles the credentials question

The site has an unresolved credibility strip, and the assumption has been that it
needs a licence class, a licence number and a canopy figure. **His own writing
says that is not what he thinks proves anything.**

> "I see a few educators out here that are charging for information just like me
> and they may be decent with explanations and teaching which is definitely an
> important half of the equation but I don't see them backed up with the work!
> They usually feature some random up close up nug shots, shots of gardens with
> somewhat questionable health and graphs and words. It's hard to even tell if
> they are their own pictures as there are no distinguishing features that let
> you know it's legit. **You can see the same rooms every time in our shots, over
> and over again with new plants and the same consistent success and quality.**"

So his proof is: **the same rooms, photographed repeatedly over time, with the
plants changing and the quality not.** Plus the deltas he can state. From these
posts alone, and all of them his own words:

- Operating 18 months at the time of the runoff guide, and "we've improved nearly
  every run in every aspect", naming plant health, total cannabinoids, yield,
  terpene content, process efficiency and cost of goods sold.
- Average THC per strain up roughly 25 to 30% since moving to lower EC.
- Lower nutrient cost with yield held and quality up, after dialling salts back.
- Runs foliar analysis on his own crop and has published the results.
- Paid for five hours of consulting with Slow Nickel and did the reading.
- An employee attended the Utah State commercial cannabis cultivation programme.
- Sells education to growers who keep paying for it.

**Every one of those is checkable against his feed, and none of it requires him
to send paperwork.** That unblocks a strip that has been waiting on him twice.

Two cautions. Ask before publishing a specific figure, because these came from
posts of a particular date and the numbers move. And run any licence claim past
him regardless: he operates in a regulated market and what a licensed operator
may say about their licence is his lawyer's question, not ours.

## Writing for him, as a checklist

- [ ] At least one real cultivation number on the page, of the kind a grower can check.
- [ ] No flat promise of a result. What it depends on, named.
- [ ] "We", not a voiceless brand.
- [ ] Nothing that reads as a sales pitch dressed as education.
- [ ] At most one inversion or aphorism per page. He does not talk in them.
- [ ] Somewhere, something he does not know, or got wrong, or is still testing.
- [ ] Nothing lifted from the Patreon. He sells that.
- [ ] Read it back and ask whether a grower would believe a person wrote it.
